## What you will learn

- Express importance and emphasis.
- Use small print, thematic breaks, and line breaks appropriately.
- Display reserved characters as text.

## Before you begin

Complete the previous lesson in this module. Keep your editor and browser ready.

<Tip>Examples labelled as body snippets go inside your existing document's body, not inside the MDX source of a live website. Complete HTML documents can be saved directly as .html files. Read the explanation for any head-only or intentionally broken examples.</Tip>

## Meaningful emphasis

`strong` marks importance, seriousness, or urgency. `em` marks stress emphasis, such as a word whose stress changes a sentence's meaning. Their default bold and italic appearance is not their purpose.

`small` represents side comments or small print. Use `hr` for a thematic change, not simply to draw decoration. Use `br` when a line break is part of the content, such as an address or poem; use paragraphs for separate ideas.

## Reserved characters

The browser treats `<` as the possible start of markup and `&` as the possible start of a character reference. Write `&lt;` for a literal less-than sign and `&amp;` for an ampersand. `&gt;` displays a greater-than sign. Ordinary repeated spaces collapse in normal text.

## Worked example

```html
<h1>Study session</h1>
<p><strong>Registration closes on Friday.</strong></p>
<p>Please bring <em>your own</em> notebook.</p>
<p>Meet at:<br>Learning Room<br>Main Campus</p>
<hr>
<p>The expression 2 &lt; 5 is true.</p>
<p><small>This is a fictional announcement.</small></p>
```

The deadline has importance, while “your own” has stress. The thematic break separates the announcement from a practice fact.

<Note>A coloured or bold appearance alone does not give content semantic meaning.</Note>
