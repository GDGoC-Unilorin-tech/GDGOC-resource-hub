## What you will learn

- Run a repeatable review.
- Keep evidence of defects and repairs.
- Explain the limits of automated and manual checks.

## Before you begin

Complete the Intermediate portfolio milestone and earlier lessons in this module.

<Tip>Examples labelled as body snippets go inside your existing document's body, not inside the MDX source of a live website. Complete HTML documents can be saved directly as .html files. Read the explanation for any head-only or intentionally broken examples.</Tip>

## Audit systematically

Review each page rather than only the home page. Start with document structure, then headings, regions, unique IDs, links, images, tables, forms, and media. Inspect source and the parsed DOM.

Distinguish three checks: source review looks for markup mistakes; DevTools inspection shows the parsed DOM and accessibility information; Lighthouse reports selected automated issues. Neither DevTools nor Lighthouse is a full HTML conformance checker. If you have not run a dedicated conformance checker, record that limitation instead of claiming validated HTML. A syntactically valid page can still have poor alternative text or inaccessible journeys.

## Test actual journeys

Navigate with Tab and Shift+Tab. Activate links and disclosure controls. Try invalid and valid fictional form entries. Confirm captions and transcripts. Test a narrow viewport and record image overflow for the later CSS stage rather than claiming an HTML-only site has a polished responsive layout.

## Run a browser review

1. Serve the portfolio locally and open a page in Chrome.
2. Open DevTools, choose Elements, and compare the page regions with your saved source. Use the Accessibility pane to inspect a link or form control's name and role.
3. Open Network, reload, and inspect failed image, media, or page requests. Repair paths in the source and retest.
4. Open the Lighthouse panel, select Accessibility, and run a report. Investigate each relevant result; keep the report with the page name and date. Panel locations can vary by browser version.
5. Close DevTools and complete a keyboard-only journey. Automated results do not replace this step.

If a tool is unavailable, record **Not tested** and describe the manual checks you performed. Do not invent a score. The course's Google references are collected on the Further Learning page.

## Keep a review log

Record the page, reproducible problem, expected result, repair, and retest. A screenshot alone does not explain the HTML decision. Maintain an honest list of remaining issues.

## Try it and observe

### Reproduce a defect before repairing it

Work on a backup. Change a skip-link href to a nonexistent fragment, reproduce the failure with the keyboard, and write the exact steps in your audit. Repair the href in the saved file, reload, and repeat the same steps. Audit all pages, including practice-result.html and any embedded notes. Mark checks you could not perform as Not tested.

## Practice challenge

Audit every portfolio page, repair at least one deliberately introduced defect, and document checks of links, keyboard operation, images, forms, and media. Demonstrate a visitor journey and state two review limitations.

Attempt the task first. Save your source and inspect the result in your browser. Record what you expected and what you observed.

<Accordion title="Need a hint?">
Use the checklist in small passes: first links, then structure, then interaction and media.
</Accordion>

## Check your understanding

Does a high Lighthouse score prove full accessibility or valid HTML? What makes a repair reproducible?

Write your answer before opening the explanation.

<Accordion title="Check your answer">

No. Automated audits cover a subset of issues and are not HTML conformance validation. A reproducible repair records the page, setup, steps, expected and observed result, source change, and the outcome of repeating the same check.

</Accordion>
