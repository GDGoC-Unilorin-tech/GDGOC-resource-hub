## What you will learn

- Identify the main content regions.
- Use native semantic elements.
- Distinguish structure from layout.

## Before you begin

Complete the Beginner stage project and earlier lessons in this module. Work in a copy of your growing portfolio.

<Tip>Examples labelled as body snippets go inside your existing document's body, not inside the MDX source of a live website. Complete HTML documents can be saved directly as .html files. Read the explanation for any head-only or intentionally broken examples.</Tip>

## Describe the regions of a page

`header` introduces a page or section. `nav` identifies a major navigation region. `main` contains the page's primary content; this course uses one visible main per page. `footer` contains closing information about its page or section.

These elements communicate meaning; they do not automatically arrange content into columns. A `header` can occur inside an article as well as at page level, so context matters.

Use `div` when you genuinely need a neutral grouping and no semantic element fits. Do not replace every div with section mechanically, and do not add redundant ARIA roles to native elements.

## Worked example

```html
<header>
  <p>Amina's portfolio</p>
  <nav aria-label="Main">
    <a href="index.html">Home</a>
    <a href="about.html">About</a>
  </nav>
</header>
<main id="main-content">
  <h1>Welcome to my portfolio</h1>
  <p>I am learning frontend development.</p>
</main>
<footer><p>Created for HTML practice.</p></footer>
```

This body snippet separates navigation, primary content, and closing information. aria-label gives the navigation an accessible name; it does not change its visual appearance.

<Note>Native semantics help accessibility, but do not by themselves make a website accessible.</Note>

## Try it and observe

### Inspect meaning, not appearance

Open your home page, right-click its heading, and choose Inspect. Find the enclosing `main`, then locate `nav` and `footer`. In the Accessibility pane, inspect their computed roles. Change one wrapper from `main` to `div` in a disposable copy: the appearance may stay the same even though the primary-content landmark is lost. Restore it in your saved source.

## Practice challenge

Refactor your home page into header, nav, main, and footer. Keep the same content and working links. Explain why each element fits.

Attempt the task first. Save your source and inspect the result in your browser. Record what you expected and what you observed.

## Check your understanding

Does replacing every `div` with `section` improve a page? Why does this course use one visible `main`?

Write your answer before opening the explanation.

<Accordion title="Check your answer">

No. A section should represent a theme, usually with a heading. Neutral grouping can remain a div. One visible main region identifies the page’s primary content; headers and footers may also belong to individual articles.
