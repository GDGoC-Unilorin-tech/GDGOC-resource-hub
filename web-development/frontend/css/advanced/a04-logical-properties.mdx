## Understand the idea

Physical properties such as margin-left always refer to a physical side. Logical properties such as margin-inline-start follow the writing mode and direction. In ordinary horizontal English, inline runs left to right and block runs top to bottom; other writing systems can differ.

Use padding-inline and padding-block for paired spacing, inline-size for width along the inline axis, and border-inline-start for an accent that follows text direction. Logical properties reduce duplicate direction-specific rules, but they do not translate content or guarantee a usable internationalised interface.

Use the HTML dir attribute to communicate direction when appropriate. Test it explicitly; do not reverse text or DOM order with CSS. Keep icons and meaning-sensitive imagery under review rather than mirroring everything automatically.

## Worked example

### HTML body

```html
<h1>Direction-aware note</h1>
<aside class="note"><h2>Practice reminder</h2><p>Read, build, and review.</p></aside>
```

### styles.css

```css
.note { padding-block: 1rem; padding-inline: 1.5rem; border-inline-start: 4px solid #174ea6; max-inline-size: 40rem; margin-inline: auto; background: #e8f0fe; color: #202124; }
```

## Try it and observe

Add dir="rtl" to the note in your test file. The accent should move to the inline start on the right. Restore the direction appropriate to the actual text after the experiment.

## Practice challenge

Make the accent 6px wide and use 2rem inline padding. Keep all spacing and sizing declarations logical. Test both direction settings.

Save your changes, reload, and compare the observed result with your prediction. Keep a short note of what changed and why.

<Accordion title="Need a hint?">

Change border-inline-start and padding-inline; do not add a separate border-right rule.

</Accordion>

## Check your understanding

Does inline always mean horizontal?

<Accordion title="Check your answer">

No. Inline follows the line of text in the active writing mode. Logical properties adapt to that direction rather than always meaning left and right.
